\begin{abstract}
Barrett reduction over $\F_2$ is especially cheap when an irreducible
polynomial $f$ of degree $n$ satisfies
$\deg(f-x^n)\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor$.  Daniel Lemire asked whether such a
polynomial exists in every degree. We translate the condition into a question about
one ray class and then count that class with the polynomial von Mangoldt
function. Most of the resulting character sum can now be bounded. The part
we cannot bound is a signed sum over characters of high $2$-power order. We
state the needed bound and prove that it would finish the argument. Separate
certified tests cover every degree through 3000. A companion document records
what has been tried, what is provably blocked, and what might still work.
\end{abstract}

\section{The reduction (\proved)}

\textbf{1. Translate the condition.} For $j\geq0$ set
$\E_j=(1+x\F_2[x])/(x^{j+1})$, $|\E_j|=2^j$, and for monic $F$ let
$\langle F\rangle_j=x^{\deg F}F(x^{-1})\bmod x^{j+1}$. Then
$\deg(f-x^n)\leq\lfloor n/2\rfloor$ if and only if $\langle f\rangle_\ell=1$:
the conjecture asks whether one ray class contains a prime of degree $n$.
Reversing $f$ gives the arithmetic-progression form --- a prime $P$ of degree
$n$ with $P\equiv1\pmod{x^{\lceil n/2\rceil}}$ --- so this is Linnik's problem
with exponent exactly $2$ at a prime-power modulus, equivalently Legendre's
conjecture for $\F_2[t]$. In Witt-vector terms (Katz's splitting
$\E_j\cong\prod_{k\text{ odd}\leq j}\mathbb Z/2^{e_k}$,
$e_k=\lfloor\log_2(j/k)\rfloor+1$) the identity class is the locus where all
odd-power Galois-ring traces vanish to these dyadic precisions.

\textbf{2. Count prime powers.} With
$N_j(b)=\sum_{\deg F=n,\ \langle F\rangle_j=b}\Lambda(F)$, logarithmic
differentiation gives this weighted count exactly. At the odd endpoint
$n=2\ell+1$, $N_\ell(1)=1+nI_n(1)$; at $n=2\ell+2$ odd proper powers do not
meet the identity class and a direct count bounds the rest. Either way it
suffices to prove
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mass}
 N_\ell(1)>2^{n-\ell}-2^\ell.
\end{equation}

\textbf{3. Split by conductor.} Each $\E_j\to\E_{j-1}$ has two-element fibres;
writing $H_j(b)$ for the difference of the two child populations above $b$,
repeated binary splitting telescopes to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:haar}
 2^\ell N_\ell(e)=2^n+\sum_{j=1}^{\ell}\varepsilon_j(e)2^{j-1}H_j(b_j(e)),
 \qquad\varepsilon_j(e)\in\{\pm1\},
\end{equation}
and for the primitive characters $X_j$ of conductor $j$,
$H_j(b)=2^{1-j}\sum_{\chi\in X_j}\overline{\chi(b)}S_n(\chi)$ with
$|H_j(b)|\leq(j-1)2^{\lceil n/2\rceil}$ by the function-field Riemann
hypothesis. That is strong enough at small conductors; applied separately at
every conductor it overshoots by a factor of order $\ell$. \emph{The whole
problem is that factor.}

\textbf{4. A characteristic-two bilinear bound.} The inverse-energy estimate
that Type-I/II arguments need does hold in characteristic two, including
products wrapping modulo $x^r$: group collisions by $x$-adic valuation, turn
each group into a bounded-degree factorisation problem by a Pad\'e
approximation, then use lift counts and divisor bounds. It does not finish the
problem, because applying it one term at a time destroys the signs in
\eqref{eq:haar}. (The published Type-I/II theorems of this shape assume odd
characteristic; see \S5.)

\textbf{5. Add the large conductors before taking absolute values.} Let
$c=\lceil\log_2\ell\rceil$, $a=\ell-c-1$,
$W_{\ell,n}=2^{\lceil n/2\rceil}\sum_{1\leq j<a}(j-1)2^{j-1}$ and
$B_{\ell,n}=2^{2\ell}-W_{\ell,n}>0$. For the identity class the terms
telescope:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rel}
 C_{\ell,n}:=\sum_{j=a}^{\ell}2^{j-1}H_j(1)
 =2^\ell N_\ell(1)-2^{a-1}N_{a-1}(1),
\end{equation}
and it is enough to prove $C_{\ell,n}>-B_{\ell,n}$, which we call (REL): the
small conductors contribute at least $-W$, so (REL) gives
$2^\ell N_\ell(1)-2^n>-W-B=-2^{2\ell}$, which is \eqref{eq:mass}.

\textbf{6. Split the remaining characters by order.} For $s\geq0$ put
$h_{j,s}=2^{j-\lfloor j/2^s\rfloor}$ and
$P_{j,s}=\sum_{g\in2^s\E_j}N_j(g)$; orthogonality gives the sum over
characters of conductor $j$ and exact order $2^s$ as
$T_{j,s}=h_{j,s}P_{j,s}-h_{j,s-1}P_{j,s-1}-h_{j-1,s}P_{j-1,s}
+h_{j-1,s-1}P_{j-1,s-1}$.
With $Q$ the largest power of two such that
$3Q\lceil\log_2\ell\rceil\leq\ell$, the Weil bound handles every order at most
$Q$, leaving $O(\log\log\ell)$ high orders per conductor --- at $\ell=200$, 47
of the 67 conductor--order pairs.

\section{What remains open}

\textbf{7. Bound the high orders (\openstatus).} For $a\leq j\leq\ell$ and
$2^s>Q$ with the corresponding character set nonempty, the following would be
enough:
\begin{equation*}\tag{HWO}
 4\ell\,|T_{j,s}(n)|\leq\#X_{j,s}(j-1)2^{\lceil n/2\rceil}.
\end{equation*}
Equivalently, without characters: with $\Delta_{j,s}=2P_{j,s}-P_{j-1,s}$,
$d_s=\lfloor(j-1)/2^s\rfloor$ and $R_{j,s}=2^{d_{s-1}-d_s}$, (HWO) is
$4\ell|R_{j,s}\Delta_{j,s}-\Delta_{j,s-1}|\leq(R_{j,s}-1)(j-1)2^{\lceil
n/2\rceil}$ when $2^{s-1}\nmid j$, and the direct sparse-discrepancy bound
$4\ell|\Delta_{j,s}|\leq(j-1)2^{\lceil n/2\rceil}$ when $2^{s-1}\mid j$ but
$2^s\nmid j$. So a Witt-precision comparison addresses only the nonresonant
layers, and resonant ones occur in the target range ($j=200$, $2^s=16$).
Then
\[
 \boxed{\text{(HWO), or another proof of (REL)}}
 \Longrightarrow\text{(REL)}\Longrightarrow\eqref{eq:mass}
 \Longrightarrow\text{a prime in the identity class}.
\]
A proof of (HWO) for $\ell\geq200$ and $n\in\{2\ell+1,2\ell+2\}$ proves the
conjecture for all $n$; every degree $n\leq3000$ carries an exhibited,
doubly certified half-degree irreducible (\finite).

\textbf{The requirement is a dial, and it is smaller than $4\ell$.} By
orthogonality $N_\ell(1)>0$ exactly when
$|\sum_{\chi\neq1}S_n(\chi)|<2^n-2^\ell\Theta$, $\Theta$ the proper-power mass,
while Weil bounds the left side by $2^{n/2}D(l)$ with
$D(l)=\sum_{j\le l}(j-1)2^{j-1}=(l-2)2^l+2$. So the whole problem is one
aggregate saving: if that sum is at most $2^{n/2}D(l)/F$, one gets an
irreducible with $\deg(f-x^n)\le\lfloor n/2\rfloor+k$ for
$k=\log_2((\ell-2)/F)+O(1)$. Here $F=1$ is the known unconditional bound
(Hayes; sharp form Hsu, Cohen, Gao), giving $k=\log_2 n-1+O(1)$, and $k=0$ is
the conjecture --- so \emph{doubling $F$ buys exactly one coefficient}, and any
unconditional $F>1$ at $q=2$ would already be new. In this aggregate form the
requirement at $\ell=200$ is $F>140.007$ ($n$ odd) and $F>99.000$ ($n$ even),
that is $F>(\ell-2)/\kappa$ with $\kappa=2^{n/2-\ell}$, rather than the
$4\ell=800$ of (HWO): the chain's extra factor is mostly its use of the weaker
$B=2^{2\ell}-W$ in place of $2^n-2^\ell\Theta-W$. The aggregate statement also
dispenses with $a$, $c$, $Q$, $W$ and $B$ altogether and lets one layer
subsidise another, which the exact data show happening by two orders of
magnitude --- but it does not change the shape of what must be proved.

\section{Two sufficient statements, and what is measured}

\textbf{(CYL), the smallest known.} Let $K=\ker(\E_\ell\to\E_{a-1})$,
elementary abelian of order $2^{\ell-a+1}$, and for a character $\psi$ of $K$
let $A_\psi=\sum_{\deg F=n,\ \langle F\rangle_{a-1}=1}\Lambda(F)\psi(\langle
F\rangle_\ell)$ be the prime mass of a cylinder twisted by a sign pattern of
the coefficients of $x^{n-a},\dots,x^{n-\ell}$. Fourier inversion on $K$ and
$B_{\ell,n}\geq2^{2\ell-1}$ give: $|A_\psi|<2^{\ell-1}$ for every
$\psi\neq1$ implies (REL). Weil gives only
$|A_\psi|\leq(\ell-1)2^{\lceil n/2\rceil}$, a factor $4(\ell-1)$ short; the
random model predicts $|A_\psi|\approx2^{(n-a+1)/2}\sqrt\ell$. Exact
computation for $12\leq\ell\leq24$ at both endpoints matches that prediction
within $8\%$ and finds (CYL) true from $\ell=16$ ($n$ odd) and $\ell=18$ ($n$
even) on, $\max_\psi|A_\psi|/2^{\ell-1}$ falling to $0.09$ and $0.15$ at
$\ell=24$, and likewise for all $2^{a-1}$ translated cylinders from
$\ell=20$. This is finite evidence, not a proof. It does resist the one
disproof route available at fixed $q$: since $|K|<8\ell$, a Plancherel
forcing argument gains at most $\sqrt{8\ell}$ against a root-mean-square that
is $\Theta(\ell2^{-\ell/2})$ of the threshold, and no $A_\psi$ vanishes at any
computed endpoint (for $\ell=23$, $n=47$ the total mass is odd, which forces
it).

\textbf{The geometric face, re-posed.} By Katz the characters of exact
conductor $j$ are the $\F_2$-points of
$\mathrm{Prim}_j\cong\mathbb G_m\times\mathbb A^{j-1}$, carrying a lisse
$L_{\mathrm{univ}}$ of rank $j-1$ whose Frobenius traces are the $S_n(\chi)$;
the conductor-$j$ sum is a Lefschetz trace of the Adams twist
$\Xi_n(L_{\mathrm{univ}})$. Deligne's weight bounds show that a bound on the
sum of Betti numbers \emph{alone} can never give (HWO): with the top
cohomology present one would need a Betti sum below $1/4$. What is needed is a
\emph{degree} statement --- the top $\approx6+2\log_2C$ cohomology groups must
vanish --- together with a Betti bound $\lesssim2^{j/2}$. Two facts narrow
this. Geometric monodromy contains $SL(j-1)$ for every $j\geq4$ in
characteristic two (Katz, IMRN 2013, Thm.~5.1; $j=3$ is finite, Gorodetsky
2019), which forces $H^{2j}_c=0$ for $j\geq4$; and an exact $L$-function
computation over $\F_{2^r}$ ($j\leq7$) puts the top weight at $n+j+O(1)$,
never $n+2j-O(1)$, matching the $\F_2$ layer sums at $\ell=24$. The residual
gap is the characteristic-two case of Sawin's Hypothesis~H
(arXiv:1810.01303), whose only unconditional input is vacuous at $p=2$, plus
a uniform Betti bound ($2^{O(j\log j)}$ is the best known).

\section{What is provably blocked}

Four barriers are proved, and they explain why the factor $\ell$ survives every
elementary attack. \emph{Moduli-only:} an explicit nonnegative fake population
has the true low-conductor Fourier data, all high moduli inside Weil, second
moment below the truth, and an empty identity class. \emph{Symmetry:} every
degree-preserving symmetry lies in $PGL_2(\F_2)$, only the Borel descends to
$\E_\ell$, and the orbit of the identity class under all structural symmetries
has size at most $2$. \emph{Construction:} provably irreducibility-preserving
constructions multiply the degree, so window families are lacunary and none
ever reaches a prime $n$ --- though monomial substitution
$f\mapsto f(x^t)$ never leaves the window
($f(x^t)-x^{mt}=(f-x^m)(x^t)$), so by Lidl--Niederreiter Thm.~3.35 every
in-window seed of degree $m$ and order $e$ gives in-window irreducibles of
degree $mt$ whenever every prime of $t$ divides $e$ but not $(2^m-1)/e$; from
the certified seeds this reaches $9.3\%$ of composite $n\leq10^5$, and the
case $m=2$ (Je\v{r}\'abek, 2011) is $n=2\cdot3^k$. \emph{Parity:} the window
has exact level of distribution $|W_n|$ with zero remainder, which yields an
element with all irreducible factors of degree $>(\frac14-\varepsilon)n$ and,
with Kuhn weights, one with at most three such factors --- but for
$10\leq n\leq15$ there is an explicit nonnegative rational population with the
window's exact divisor data and no irreducible in its support, so no
lower-bound sieve at that level can produce a prime; and any such certificate
applies to every window, hence would prove Legendre for $\F_2[t]$.

Over $\F_q$ the conjecture is a theorem once $q$ is large: $q>(n+1)^2/4$
suffices (Hsu; Cohen), and $q>7101p^2$ suffices for all large $n$ when $p$ is
odd (Bagshaw, arXiv:2401.10399, Cor.~2.5, via the reversal duality) --- a
threshold independent of $n$, but one that excludes characteristic two, every
prime field, and every $q=p^2$. The conjecture is open precisely because
$q=2$ is fixed.
